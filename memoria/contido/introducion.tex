\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Motivation}
Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) have been at the forefront of computer science research for the past few decades, the core concept of building artificial systems that simulate the biological behavior of the human brain remains a prominent research focus. In recent years, the capabilities of Deep Learning and transformer architectures have grown substantially, driven by their remarkable results and the commercialization of General Purpose Artificial Intelligence (GPAI). Nonetheless, these models suffer from two major shortcomings: their unsustainable energy and environmental consumption, and the massive initial training datasets they require. This severe energetic cost has given rise to initiatives such as Green AI \cite{schwartz2020green}, which advocates for computational efficiency as a primary evaluation criterion alongside model accuracy. Conversely, neuroevolutionary algorithms attempt to replicate both the mechanics of the brain and the evolutionary processes of living organisms. In particular, the NeuroEvolution of Augmented Topologies (NEAT) algorithm \cite{stanley2002} offers a paradigm shift from its predecessors, yielding excellent results for continuous, high-dimensional control tasks, as will be detailed in subsequent sections.

The practical application of this project is heavily inspired by a well-known demonstration of the NEAT algorithm navigating a Super Mario Bros. environment, an implementation its creator named MarI/O \cite{sethbling2015mario}. This demonstration clearly illustrates that NEAT does not require massive training datasets nor any prior information about the environment. MarI/O begins the simulation without knowing the controls, discovers movement through trial and error, and evolves until it can clear the level autonomously. This effectively solves the first major shortcoming regarding initial data dependency.

However, standard NEAT does not resolve the energy consumption problem on its own. To address this, this project aims to implement two biologically inspired principles: Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) \cite{gerstner2014} and Reward-Modulated Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (R-STDP) \cite{qiu2018evolving}. SNNs are built upon the premise that biological brains communicate via discrete energy "spikes". To put this into perspective, standard ANNs operate like an open tap where water (energy) flows continuously and without interruption; in contrast, adapting SNNs is akin to closing the tap to a bare minimum, allowing water to accumulate and only fall as a single drop once it reaches a specific density. Furthermore, the integration of R-STDP significantly enhances the evolutionary process. While standard NEAT focuses purely on evolving the network's topology, R-STDP focuses on dynamically evolving the synaptic weights during gameplay. This dual mechanism grants the agent greater adaptability, theoretically allowing the "brain" to generalize to new levels or similar tasks without the need to restart the training process from scratch.

Ultimately, the exploration of SNNs and R-STDP in this project aligns perfectly with the cutting edge of neuromorphic engineering. As modern technology increasingly shifts toward edge computing, autonomous robotics, and Internet of Things (IoT) devices, the demand for AI systems capable of real-time adaptation with minimal power consumption has never been higher. Therefore, validating these biological learning mechanisms within a dynamic software environment like Super Mario Bros not only addresses an immediate academic gap, but also directly contributes to the foundation of next generation, hardware deployable AI. 

\section{Objectives}

\subsection{Main objective}
The primary objective of this project is to develop an autonomous Artificial Intelligence program, commonly referred to as an ‘agent’, capable of navigating and completing levels within the Super Mario Bros environment. In this context, an agent is an independent computational entity that continuously observes its surroundings and makes decisions to achieve a specific goal. This decision-making process will be driven by Reinforcement Learning, a machine learning paradigm in which the agent learns optimal behaviors through trial, error, and the reception of environmental rewards.

To achieve this while addressing the energy inefficiencies of modern AI, the project will substitute the computationally expensive continuous neural networks traditionally used in these tasks with a biologically plausible Spiking Neural Network (SNN). Furthermore, the agent's learning mechanism will be governed by Reward-Modulated Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (R-STDP), enabling it to dynamically adapt its synaptic connections in real-time. 

Ultimately, the goal is to prove that an agent built upon these biologically inspired principles can achieve high adaptability and learning efficiency in a dynamic environment while drastically minimizing theoretical energy consumption.

\subsection{Specific Objectives}
To achieve the main objective, the project is divided into the following sub-goals:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Establish a Baseline:} Implement and train a standard NEAT algorithm to play Super Mario Bros to serve as a performance and architectural baseline.
    \item \textbf{Implement Spiking Mechanics:} Replace standard continuous activation functions (e.g., Sigmoid) with a discrete Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) \cite{gerstner2014} neuron model to simulate biological spiking behavior.
    \item \textbf{Integrate Reinforcement Learning:} Substitute random weight mutation with Reward-Modulated Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (R-STDP) to allow the agent to learn dynamically from environmental rewards (dopamine signals).
    \item \textbf{Evaluate Efficiency:} Compare the performance and theoretical computational energy consumption of the Spiking-NEAT agent against the standard NEAT baseline.
\end{itemize}

\section{Chosen approach}
To achieve the expected results, the project methodology is conceptually divided into three metaphorical pillars: the architect, the engine, and the teacher.

\textbf{The Architect (Topology):} is the foundational structure of the agent's brain is generated using NeuroEvolution of Augmented Topologies (NEAT). NEAT improves upon earlier Topology and Weight Evolving Artificial Neural Networks (TWEANNs) by utilizing historical markings (innovation numbers), speciation, and incremental growth from minimal structures. Because NEAT has been validated on complex control tasks like double pole balancing, it serves as the ideal "architect" to dynamically grow the network topology for this project.

\textbf{The Engine (Activation Function):} Standard NEAT typically employs a continuous sigmoidal activation function bounded between $[0,1]$. However, because these activation values are continuous floating-point fractions, the underlying hardware is forced to perform computationally expensive Multiply Accumulate (MAC) operations for every single synapse, at every single time step. Even if a node's activation is microscopically close to zero, the system must still execute dense matrix arithmetic across the entire network to calculate the next frame. This persistent, unconditional computation is exactly what drives the massive energy consumption problem acknowledged in the previous section. To resolve this, the network will be transformed into a Spiking Neural Network (SNN) by implementing the Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) model [2]. The LIF model is a mathematical abstraction of biological neurons focusing entirely on the timing of electrical spikes via three mechanics: integrate (accumulating incoming electrical signals), leak (the natural decay of voltage over time), and fire (emitting a binary spike when the voltage surpasses a threshold and resetting). Expanding on the previous water tap metaphor, the LIF model acts as a bucket with a small hole that fills up until the bucket is full and gets emptied (resets) after. The tap represents synaptic current (integrate), the water level represents membrane potential, the hole represents natural leakage (leak), and the overflow when the bucket fills up completely represents the spike (fire). This discrete, binary event-driven approach is highly efficient because neurons only perform calculations when a discrete spike occurs, essentially reducing continuous matrix multiplication to sparse, binary addition.

\textbf{The Teacher (Learning Rule):} In standard NEAT, synaptic weights are mutated randomly between generations, which can result in inefficient learning. To address this, the project introduces Reinforcement Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (R-STDP) as the "teacher". R-STDP acts as a biologically plausible reinforcement learning mechanism. Rather than randomly guessing weight values, R-STDP analyzes which neurons spiked immediately prior to a successful in-game action and strengthens those specific connections. By mimicking the release of dopamine in a biological brain, the model is directly encouraged to repeat productive behaviors.
